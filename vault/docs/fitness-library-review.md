## What to look for

- **Steps and cues:** would a sensible beginner end up doing this safely and correctly from the words alone? Anything missing, or wrong?
- **Mistakes and fixes:** are these the mistakes people really make, and are the fixes right?
- **Level:** is the 1-4 level (and so who is offered it) right? Beginner-rated people only see levels 1-2; intermediate 1-3; advanced 1-4; one lower in easing and lighter weeks.
- **Dosage:** sets, reps, holds and minutes for beginner / intermediate / advanced. Are they sensible for a lifestyle program (health and longevity, not bodybuilding)?
- **Equipment and setting:** is what each one needs listed correctly?
- **Safety notes:** shown on the exercise page; anything missing?

## Judgement calls I would like you to check first

1. **Jogging.** *Easy jog* is level 3 and *Run/walk intervals* level 2. People with fewer than 90 minutes of cardio in the week are never offered level 3+ cardio, whatever they rate themselves. Is that cautious enough, or too cautious?
2. **Heavier loads.** Dumbbell exercises say "light", "heavier" and so on but never give kilograms. Is that right for a general audience, or should there be a guide?
3. **Single-leg Romanian deadlift** is level 3 and also counts as balance work. Offered to intermediate-rated people. Too early?
4. **Leg press and assisted pull-up** tell people to ask gym staff to set up the first time. Enough?
5. **No pulling exercise without dumbbells or a gym.** People who list only bodyweight get no row or pull-down; the week says so and leaves the slot out. Is there a safe bodyweight pull I should add (for example a towel row)?
6. **Warm-up and cool-down** are about 5 minutes each, two exercises each, drawn from mobility and balance. Are the choices sensible as warm-ups (for example *Tandem stand* and *Single-leg stand* as balance warm-ups)?
7. **Aerobic intensity** is described with the talk test only. Fine, or should there also be a 1-10 effort scale?
8. **Anything dangerous or missing** that you would not want an older or deconditioned person doing from this wording.

## Squat (4)

### Sit-to-stand

`sit-to-stand` · level 1 (gentle) · strength · Chair · where: home · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Sit toward the front of a sturdy chair with your feet hip-width apart and slightly behind your knees.
2. Lean your chest forward from the hips until your nose is over your toes.
3. Press through your whole foot and stand tall, squeezing your glutes at the top.
4. Reach your hips back and lower slowly over about three seconds until you lightly touch the seat.
5. Pause briefly, then go again without bouncing off the seat.

**Common mistakes**

- Knees drift inwards. *Fix:* Press your knees out so they track over your second toe.
- Pushing off with your hands. *Fix:* Cross your arms, or use a higher seat until you can stand without help.

**Why form matters.** This is the movement you use to get out of any chair. Leaning forward and keeping your knees tracking over your toes loads your thighs and glutes instead of your knees and lower back.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 6-8 reps. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-10 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-12 slow reps.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Bodyweight squat.

### Bodyweight squat

`bodyweight-squat` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart and toes turned out slightly.
2. Keep your chest up and your tummy gently braced, arms out in front for balance.
3. Sit your hips back and down as if lowering into a chair, knees tracking over your toes.
4. Lower until your thighs are close to parallel, or as far as you can while keeping your heels down.
5. Press through the whole foot to stand tall.

**Common mistakes**

- Heels lift off the floor. *Fix:* Shift your weight to mid-foot and shorten the depth until it feels steady.
- Lower back rounds at the bottom. *Fix:* Keep your chest proud and stop higher; depth only counts if your back stays neutral.

**Why form matters.** Controlled depth with a neutral spine builds your thighs and glutes. Dropping fast or rounding your back moves the load onto your knees and spine.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps. Intermediate: 3 sets of 10-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 12-15 slow reps.

**Easier:** Sit-to-stand. **Harder:** Goblet squat, Leg press (machine).

### Goblet squat

`goblet-squat` · level 3 (challenging) · strength · Dumbbell · where: gym, home · needs: any of dumbbells / gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Hold one dumbbell upright against your chest with both hands cupped under the top end.
2. Set your feet slightly wider than shoulder-width with toes turned out a little.
3. Keep your elbows pointing down and the weight close to your chest as you sit down between your knees.
4. Go as deep as you can with your heels flat and your back straight.
5. Drive through your whole foot to stand, breathing out as you rise.

**Common mistakes**

- The weight drifts away from your chest. *Fix:* Tuck your elbows in and hold the dumbbell against your breastbone.
- You fold forward at the bottom. *Fix:* Use a lighter weight or lower less deeply until you can stay tall.

**Why form matters.** Holding the weight in front helps you stay upright, which keeps the load on your legs. Folding forward turns it into a lower-back exercise.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps, light weight. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-12 reps, heavier weight.

**Easier:** Bodyweight squat. **Harder:** none.

### Leg press (machine)

`leg-press` · level 3 (challenging) · strength · Leg press machine · where: gym · needs: any of gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Adjust the seat so your knees bend to about 90 degrees at the bottom, with your feet shoulder-width apart in the middle of the platform.
2. Keep your head, back and hips pressed against the seat.
3. Press the platform away until your legs are nearly straight, stopping short of locking your knees.
4. Lower slowly over about three seconds until your knees reach about 90 degrees.
5. Breathe out as you press and in as you lower.

**Common mistakes**

- Locking your knees at the top. *Fix:* Stop just short of straight so the muscles, not the joint, hold the weight.
- Hips lift or round off the seat at the bottom. *Fix:* Shorten the range and lower the weight; your hips should stay flat.

**Why form matters.** A machine supports your back, but the knees still take load. A controlled range with unlocked knees protects the joints while you build strength.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 10-12 reps, light. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps, heavier.

**Safety note shown to the user.** Start light and ask a gym staff member to set up the machine the first time.

**Easier:** Bodyweight squat. **Harder:** none.

## Hinge (3)

### Glute bridge

`glute-bridge` · level 1 (gentle) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat, hip-width apart, heels under your knees.
2. Gently draw your ribs down and tighten your tummy so your lower back is not arched.
3. Press through your heels and lift your hips until your body makes a straight line from shoulders to knees.
4. Squeeze your glutes for one to two seconds at the top.
5. Lower slowly, one bone at a time.

**Common mistakes**

- Lower back arches at the top. *Fix:* Stop when your body is in a straight line and keep your ribs down.
- Hamstrings cramp. *Fix:* Move your feet closer to your hips and push through your heels, not your toes.

**Why form matters.** The glutes are your main hip extensors, and this teaches the hip hinge in a safe position. Arching your back lets the lower back do the work instead.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps. Intermediate: 3 sets of 10-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 12-15 reps with a 2 second hold.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Dumbbell Romanian deadlift.

### Dumbbell Romanian deadlift

`dumbbell-romanian-deadlift` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Dumbbells · where: gym, home · needs: any of dumbbells / gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand tall with a dumbbell in each hand in front of your thighs, feet hip-width apart, knees soft.
2. Push your hips straight back, as if closing a car door with your bottom, letting the dumbbells slide down your thighs.
3. Keep your back flat and your shoulders back and down.
4. Lower until you feel a stretch behind your thighs, usually just below the knee.
5. Drive your hips forward to stand tall and squeeze your glutes.

**Common mistakes**

- Your back rounds. *Fix:* Use a lighter weight and shorten the range; stop where you can keep a flat back.
- You squat down instead of hinging. *Fix:* Keep your shins near vertical and think of pushing the hips back, not the knees forward.

**Why form matters.** The hinge strengthens the whole back of your body and protects your spine when you lift things in daily life. A rounded back puts that load on your discs instead.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps, light weights. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps, heavier weights.

**Easier:** Glute bridge. **Harder:** Single-leg Romanian deadlift.

### Single-leg Romanian deadlift

`single-leg-rdl` · level 3 (challenging) · strength + balance · Bodyweight (dumbbell optional) · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand next to a wall or sturdy chair so you can touch it if you wobble.
2. Balance on one leg with a soft knee and your hips square to the front.
3. Hinge forward from the hip, letting your free leg float back behind you, back flat.
4. Go down until your body is roughly parallel to the floor or you feel a stretch behind the standing leg.
5. Drive your hip forward to return. Finish all reps on one side, then swap.

**Common mistakes**

- Hips twist open. *Fix:* Keep both hip bones pointing at the floor; lift the back leg less if needed.
- Standing knee locks straight. *Fix:* Keep a slight bend throughout.

**Why form matters.** It trains the glutes and hamstrings and also challenges balance, which matters for staying steady as you get older. Twisting or locking the knee puts stress on the joint.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 5-6 reps each side, hand on support. Intermediate: 3 sets of 6-8 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps each side, light dumbbell.

**Safety note shown to the user.** Keep a wall or chair within reach.

**Easier:** Dumbbell Romanian deadlift. **Harder:** none.

## Lunge (4)

### Split squat

`split-squat` · level 1 (gentle) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Take a long step forward so your feet are in a staggered stance, hip-width apart as if on train tracks.
2. Lift your back heel and keep your torso upright; rest a hand on a wall or chair if you need to.
3. Lower straight down until your front thigh is close to parallel and your back knee heads toward the floor.
4. Keep your front knee over your ankle.
5. Press through the front foot to rise. Finish one side, then swap.

**Common mistakes**

- Stance is too short, so the front knee shoots forward. *Fix:* Step further so your front shin stays close to vertical.
- Front heel lifts. *Fix:* Shift weight to mid-foot and lengthen the step.

**Why form matters.** A stable, long stance shares the load between hip and knee. A short stance loads the front knee and makes you wobble.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 6-8 reps each side, using support. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-10 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-12 reps each side.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Reverse lunge, Lateral lunge.

### Lateral lunge

`lateral-lunge` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand with your feet together and your toes pointing forward.
2. Step one foot out wide to the side.
3. Sit your hips back and bend that knee, keeping the other leg straight and both feet flat.
4. Keep your chest up and your knee over your toes.
5. Push through the bent leg to return to standing, then repeat to the other side.

**Common mistakes**

- The bent knee falls inwards. *Fix:* Track it over your second toe.
- Rounding your back as you sit. *Fix:* Keep your chest up and reduce the step width.

**Why form matters.** Side-to-side strength in the hips and inner thighs is often neglected, and it protects you when you step sideways or catch your balance.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 5-6 reps each side. Intermediate: 3 sets of 6-8 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps each side.

**Easier:** Split squat. **Harder:** none.

### Reverse lunge

`reverse-lunge` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand tall with your feet hip-width apart and hands on your hips.
2. Step one foot back, landing on the ball of the foot.
3. Lower both knees to about 90 degrees: front knee over the ankle, back knee hovering just above the floor.
4. Keep your torso upright.
5. Push through the front foot to return to standing, then repeat on the other side.

**Common mistakes**

- Front knee collapses inward. *Fix:* Push the front knee gently out so it lines up over your second toe.
- Stepping back on a tightrope. *Fix:* Keep your feet in two lanes, hip-width apart, for stability.

**Why form matters.** Stepping back keeps your weight over the front leg, which is gentler on the knee than stepping forward. Narrow feet make it a balance test instead of a strength exercise.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 6-8 reps each side. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-10 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-12 reps each side, holding dumbbells.

**Easier:** Split squat. **Harder:** Walking lunge.

### Walking lunge

`walking-lunge` · level 3 (challenging) · strength · Bodyweight (dumbbells optional) · where: home, gym, outdoor · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Find about 6 to 8 metres of clear space.
2. Take a long step forward and lower until both knees are around 90 degrees.
3. Push off the front foot and bring your back foot through into the next step.
4. Keep your torso upright and your steps long enough that your front shin stays near vertical.
5. Walk to the end, turn and walk back.

**Common mistakes**

- Steps are too short and the front knee goes far over the toes. *Fix:* Take longer steps.
- Rushing so you lose control. *Fix:* Slow down and pause briefly at the bottom of each step.

**Why form matters.** The extra steps add a coordination and balance challenge. Rushing sends the body forward over the front knee and wastes the strength benefit.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 rounds of 6 steps each leg. Intermediate: 3 rounds of 8 steps each leg. Advanced: 3 rounds of 10 steps each leg, holding dumbbells.

**Easier:** Reverse lunge. **Harder:** none.

## Push (4)

### Wall push-up

`wall-push-up` · level 1 (gentle) · strength · Wall · where: home, gym, outdoor · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand an arm's length from a wall with your hands on it at shoulder height, slightly wider than your shoulders.
2. Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels.
3. Bend your elbows to about 45 degrees from your body and bring your chest toward the wall.
4. Press away until your arms are straight.
5. Keep your neck long and breathe steadily.

**Common mistakes**

- Hips sag or stick out. *Fix:* Squeeze your glutes and gently tighten your tummy.
- Elbows flare out wide. *Fix:* Tuck them to about 45 degrees; this protects your shoulders.

**Why form matters.** The right elbow angle loads the chest and arms while keeping the shoulder joint safe. Flared elbows pinch the shoulder.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps. Intermediate: 3 sets of 10-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 12-15 reps.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Incline push-up.

### Incline push-up

`incline-push-up` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Bench, counter or sturdy stairs · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Place your hands on a sturdy bench, counter or stair, slightly wider than your shoulders.
2. Walk your feet back until your body is in a straight line.
3. Lower your chest toward the edge, with elbows at about 45 degrees.
4. Press back up without letting your hips sag.
5. The lower the surface, the harder it gets.

**Common mistakes**

- The surface slides or tilts. *Fix:* Use something fixed, like a countertop pushed against a wall.
- Head drops forward. *Fix:* Keep your neck in line with your spine and look at the floor a little ahead of your hands.

**Why form matters.** A raised surface lets you practise the full push-up pattern at a load you can control. Sagging hips turn it into a back exercise.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 6-8 reps. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 12-15 reps.

**Easier:** Wall push-up. **Harder:** Push-up.

### Push-up

`push-up` · level 3 (challenging) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Place your hands under your shoulders or slightly wider, with your body in a straight line from head to heels. Knees can stay down if needed.
2. Squeeze your glutes and gently tighten your tummy.
3. Lower as one piece until your chest is close to the floor, elbows at about 45 degrees.
4. Press the floor away until your arms are straight.
5. Breathe in on the way down and out as you press.

**Common mistakes**

- Hips sag or pike up. *Fix:* Keep a straight line; drop to your knees if you cannot hold it.
- Only moving the head and shoulders. *Fix:* Lower the whole body together.

**Why form matters.** A full push-up trains the chest, shoulders, arms and trunk together. Sagging hips or half reps cut the benefit and strain the lower back.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 4-6 reps (knees down if needed). Intermediate: 3 sets of 6-10 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-15 reps.

**Easier:** Incline push-up. **Harder:** Slow-tempo push-up.

### Slow-tempo push-up

`slow-tempo-push-up` · level 4 (advanced) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Set up as for a normal push-up with a straight body line.
2. Lower for a slow count of three.
3. Pause for one second just above the floor.
4. Press up smoothly and strongly.
5. Stop the set when you can no longer keep the tempo or your body line.

**Common mistakes**

- Speeding up on the way down. *Fix:* Count out loud: one, two, three.
- Hips sag as you tire. *Fix:* End the set there; quality beats extra reps.

**Why form matters.** Slowing the lowering phase makes the muscles work longer for the same number of reps, building strength without needing weights.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 3-5 reps. Intermediate: 3 sets of 5-8 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps.

**Easier:** Push-up. **Harder:** none.

## Pull (4)

### Supported dumbbell row

`supported-dumbbell-row` · level 1 (gentle) · strength · Dumbbell, bench or chair · where: gym, home · needs: any of dumbbells / gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Place one hand and the same-side knee on a bench, or one hand on a sturdy chair with the other foot back.
2. Keep your back flat and let the dumbbell hang below your shoulder.
3. Pull your elbow toward your hip, squeezing your shoulder blade toward your spine.
4. Lower slowly until your arm is straight.
5. Finish all reps on one side, then swap.

**Common mistakes**

- Twisting your torso to lift the weight. *Fix:* Keep your shoulders square to the floor and use a lighter dumbbell.
- Shrugging toward your ear. *Fix:* Keep your shoulder down and away from your ear.

**Why form matters.** Rows strengthen the muscles between your shoulder blades that hold your posture and balance all the pushing you do. Twisting moves the work away from them.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps each side, light. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-12 reps each side, heavier.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Bent-over dumbbell row, Lat pulldown (machine).

### Bent-over dumbbell row

`bent-over-dumbbell-row` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Dumbbells · where: gym, home · needs: any of dumbbells / gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Stand with your feet hip-width apart, a dumbbell in each hand.
2. Hinge forward from the hips to about 45 degrees, back flat, knees soft.
3. Let the dumbbells hang below your shoulders.
4. Row your elbows back toward your ribs, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
5. Lower under control.

**Common mistakes**

- Your back rounds. *Fix:* Use lighter weights and hinge less; your back should stay flat like a table.
- Jerking the weights up. *Fix:* Slow down. Lift for one second, lower for two.

**Why form matters.** A held hinge position builds back endurance at the same time. A rounded or swinging back puts strain on the spine.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps, light. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 10-12 reps, heavier.

**Easier:** Supported dumbbell row. **Harder:** none.

### Lat pulldown (machine)

`lat-pulldown` · level 2 (moderate) · strength · Cable machine · where: gym · needs: any of gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Sit with your thighs snug under the pads and take a grip slightly wider than your shoulders.
2. Lean back very slightly with your chest lifted.
3. Pull the bar to your upper chest by driving your elbows down and back.
4. Return slowly until your arms are straight, keeping your shoulders down.
5. Breathe out as you pull.

**Common mistakes**

- Pulling the bar behind your neck. *Fix:* Always pull it in front, to your upper chest.
- Swinging your body to move the weight. *Fix:* Lower the weight and keep your torso still.

**Why form matters.** It builds the large back muscles you need for pulling and for the pull-up later. Pulling behind your neck stresses the shoulders and neck.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 8-10 reps, light. Intermediate: 3 sets of 8-12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps, heavier.

**Easier:** Supported dumbbell row. **Harder:** Assisted pull-up (machine).

### Assisted pull-up (machine)

`assisted-pull-up` · level 3 (challenging) · strength · Assisted pull-up machine · where: gym · needs: any of gym · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Set the assistance so you can complete your reps with control; more assistance makes it easier.
2. Grip the bar slightly wider than your shoulders and start with your arms long and shoulders down.
3. Pull your chest toward the bar, leading with your elbows.
4. Lower over about three seconds to straight arms.
5. Stop the set when you cannot keep control.

**Common mistakes**

- Kicking or swinging. *Fix:* Add more assistance and keep your legs still.
- Half reps. *Fix:* Go from fully straight arms to chin over the bar, or as far as you can control.

**Why form matters.** A full pull-up is one of the best upper-body strength tests. The machine lets you build up to it while keeping shoulders and elbows safe.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 3-5 reps, high assistance. Intermediate: 3 sets of 5-8 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 6-10 reps, low assistance.

**Safety note shown to the user.** Stop if you feel pain in your shoulder or elbow.

**Easier:** Lat pulldown (machine). **Harder:** none.

## Core (4)

### Dead bug

`dead-bug` · level 1 (gentle) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Lie on your back with your arms pointing to the ceiling and your hips and knees bent at 90 degrees.
2. Gently press your lower back toward the floor.
3. Slowly lower one arm overhead and the opposite leg toward the floor without arching your back.
4. Return and swap sides.
5. Breathe out as you lower.

**Common mistakes**

- Lower back arches off the floor. *Fix:* Move less far and only lower your legs.
- Holding your breath. *Fix:* Breathe out as you lower and in as you return.

**Why form matters.** It teaches your deep tummy muscles to stabilise your spine while your limbs move, which is how they work in daily life. Arching removes that benefit.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 5-6 reps each side. Intermediate: 3 sets of 6-8 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps each side.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Bird dog.

### Bird dog

`bird-dog` · level 2 (moderate) · strength + balance · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Start on hands and knees with your hands under your shoulders and knees under your hips.
2. Keep your back flat like a table.
3. Reach one arm and the opposite leg out until they are in line with your body.
4. Pause for two seconds without letting your hips twist.
5. Return and swap sides.

**Common mistakes**

- Lifting the leg too high and arching the back. *Fix:* Raise your leg only to hip height.
- Hips twisting open. *Fix:* Imagine a glass of water sitting on your lower back.

**Why form matters.** It trains the back muscles and the deep tummy muscles to hold your spine steady while a limb moves.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 sets of 5-6 reps each side. Intermediate: 3 sets of 6-8 reps each side. Advanced: 3 sets of 8-10 reps each side with a 3 second hold.

**Easier:** Dead bug. **Harder:** Forearm plank.

### Forearm plank

`forearm-plank` · level 3 (challenging) · strength · Bodyweight · where: home, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Place your elbows under your shoulders with your forearms flat on the floor.
2. Extend your legs with your feet hip-width apart, or keep your knees down to begin.
3. Squeeze your glutes and gently draw your belly in so your body makes a straight line.
4. Hold, breathing steadily.
5. Stop when your hips start to sag.

**Common mistakes**

- Hips sag. *Fix:* Drop to your knees or shorten the hold.
- Hips pike up. *Fix:* Lower them until your body is in one line.

**Why form matters.** A good plank trains the whole trunk to resist sagging. A sagging plank loads the lower back instead.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 3 holds of 10-15 seconds (knees down if needed). Intermediate: 3 holds of 20-30 seconds. Advanced: 3 holds of 30-45 seconds.

**Easier:** Bird dog. **Harder:** Side plank.

### Brisk walk

`brisk-walk` · level 1 (gentle) · cardio · Shoes · where: outdoor, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. Walk at a pace that lifts your breathing but still lets you speak in short sentences.
2. Stand tall, look about ten metres ahead and swing your arms naturally.
3. Land softly with a natural stride; quicker, shorter steps beat long strides.
4. Build up the time gradually from week to week.

**Common mistakes**

- Overstriding, with the front foot far ahead. *Fix:* Take shorter, quicker steps.
- Slouching and looking at the ground. *Fix:* Look ahead and keep your chest open.

**Why form matters.** Walking is the base of your aerobic fitness and is gentle on joints. Good posture and a short stride keep it comfortable for long sessions.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 15-25 minutes at a pace where you can chat. Intermediate: 25-45 minutes at a brisk pace. Advanced: 40-60 minutes at a brisk pace, including hills.

**Easier:** none. **Harder:** Run/walk intervals, Trail walk, Treadmill incline walk.

### Run/walk intervals

`run-walk-intervals` · level 2 (moderate) · cardio · Shoes · where: outdoor, gym · needs: nothing · roles: main

**Steps**

1. After your warm-up, jog gently for one minute.
2. Walk briskly for two minutes to recover.
3. Repeat the cycle for the time planned.
4. Keep the jog slow enough that you can talk.
5. As you get fitter, lengthen the jog and shorten the walk.

**Common mistakes**

- Jogging too fast. *Fix:* Slow down. If you cannot talk, it is too fast.
- Skipping the walk breaks. *Fix:* The walk breaks are the plan: they let you keep going longer.

**Why form matters.** Short bursts of jogging build running fitness with less impact on your joints than continuous running.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 15-20 minutes: 1 min jog, 2 min walk. Intermediate: 20-30 minutes: 2 min jog, 1-2 min walk. Advanced: 30-40 minutes: 3-4 min jog, 1 min walk.

**Safety note shown to the user.** Choose softer surfaces such as grass or tracks where you can.

**Easier:** Brisk walk. **Harder:** Easy jog.

### Trail walk

### Tandem stand

`tandem-stand` · level 1 (gentle) · balance · Wall nearby · where: home, gym, outdoor · needs: nothing · roles: main, warmup

**Steps**

1. Stand next to a wall or sturdy bench.
2. Place one foot directly in front of the other, heel to toe.

**Common mistakes**

- Looking at your feet. *Fix:* Pick a spot on the wall at eye level.

**Dosage.** Beginner: 2 holds of 15-20 seconds each side. Intermediate: 3 holds of 20-30 seconds each side. Advanced: 3 holds of 30-45 seconds each side.
